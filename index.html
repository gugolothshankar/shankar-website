About Me

Who I Am

I am Guguloth Shankar, a PhD Research Scholar in the Department of Liberal Arts at IIT Hyderabad, where I specialise in Social and Cultural Anthropology. My doctoral research is rooted in long-term ethnographic fieldwork and explores themes of caste, identity, and cultural politics in contemporary India.

I am interested in understanding how communities negotiate their identities, cultural practices, and socio-political aspirations in the context of development, displacement, and social transformation. My approach integrates fieldwork methods with critical theoretical frameworks from anthropology, sociology, and postcolonial studies.

Prior to joining IIT Hyderabad, I completed my postgraduate studies in Anthropology and have been actively engaged with field research in central and southern India.

Research Interests:

  • Cultural Anthropology
  • Social Anthropology
  • Caste & Identity
  • Ethnography
  • Tribal Studies
  • Postcolonial Theory
  • South Asian Studies
  • Development & Displacement

Theory & Method

My theoretical framework draws on practice theory, postcolonial anthropology, and subaltern studies. Methodologically, I use participant observation, oral history interviews, archival research, and visual documentation to produce thick descriptions and theoretically-grounded analysis.

🌍

Broader Interests

Beyond my dissertation, I am interested in the anthropology of development and displacement, ritual and cosmology in South Asia, the politics of recognition and rights for scheduled tribes, and the ethics and politics of ethnographic practice in contemporary India.
